The Hardest Parts of Traveling by Canoe

Long days, heavy gear, wind, and changing conditions made the journey more demanding than it looked from shore.

Moving a loaded canoe

A canoe loaded with camping equipment and supplies handles differently from an empty one. Portages and shallow stretches could turn what looked like an easy section on the map into a slow, physical day.

Wind and open water

Large lakes were some of the most intimidating sections. On Lake Winnibigoshish, wind made the crossing feel very different from paddling a sheltered river channel. I had to respect the conditions rather than my schedule.

The mental challenge

Spending long days alone meant making decisions without anyone else to share the responsibility. When I was tired, the most useful approach was to focus on the next safe stretch instead of worrying about the entire distance ahead.
